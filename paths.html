Study paths

Guided walkthroughs that chain the site's tools into a method. Each step links to a live page; nothing here adds interpretation — the paths only order the evidence.

Path 1 · Follow a root through the Qur'an

Take r-ḥ-m (mercy) from a single verse to its full corpus distribution.

  1. Read it in context: al-Fatihah 1:1 — two of the four words carry the root.
  2. Open the root's profile: r-ḥ-m on Roots — 339 occurrences, derived forms, Makki/Madani split, every verse reference.
  3. See what it travels with: the co-occurring roots section on the same page — gh-f-r (forgiveness) is its most frequent verse-level companion.
  4. Compare passages where it clusters: al-Fatihah vs the opening of Maryam in Compare's Passages mode.

Path 2 · Profile a surah before reading it

Build a structural picture of a surah from the data, then read it with that frame.

  1. On Navigate, open the Profile panel for a surah (try 103, al-'Asr): verse count, distinct roots, top roots, chronological period.
  2. Check its length band against the era pattern on Numbers (verse length by period).
  3. Read it end to end on Read at Study depth (press 2) with word-by-word morphology on.
  4. Test your structural reading in the al-'Asr outline exercise.

Path 3 · Trace a claim to its source

The site's core discipline: never take a number on trust — including ours.

  1. Pick a claim on Numbers, for example the top-five root frequencies.
  2. Click its badge (●): the popover shows the full Chicago-style citation of the source.
  3. Follow the source entry on Sources to the primary corpus at corpus.quran.com and check one number yourself.
  4. Run a triangulation prompt from Validation against an independent tool, and see whether it converges on the same figure.

Path 4 · Study a theme end to end

Move from a life theme to the passages where its vocabulary clusters, and form your own reading of what they agree on.

  1. Pick a gateway on Themes (try Forgiveness) and note its root families — the gateway is lexical, a starting point rather than an index.
  2. Open two or three of its key passages on the Read page and read each whole discourse around the verse, not just the verse itself.
  3. Use the theme card's "top two passages side by side" link to see, in Compare, which roots the passages actually share.
  4. Follow the theme's most frequent root into Roots: where else does it cluster, and in which period? Write down what the passages agree on and where they differ.

Path 5 · Test a Khan outline against the site's own computed signals

Propose your own structure, reveal Dr. Khan's published outline, then see what the site's mechanical tools find — or don't find — in the same surah.

  1. Work through the al-'Asr outline exercise: propose your own verse-by-verse structure before revealing Khan's.
  2. Open the same surah's Dossier. Its Structure section shows the site's own computed discursive pivots, rhetorical features, and ring-composition test for this exact surah; its Rhyme and Formulas sections show the verse-ending pattern and recurring-phrase data. Not every mechanical detector finds something in every surah — an empty section is itself a datum, not a gap.
  3. Follow whichever signal caught your eye to its full method and corpus-wide result on Patterns.
  4. Return to Read at Study depth and write in the discovery worksheet where Khan's outline and the site's computed signals agree, diverge, or simply don't overlap.

Step checkmarks are saved in this browser only and are cleared by the settings panel's "Clear preferences".
