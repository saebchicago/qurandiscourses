Exercises

Practice reading the Qur'an structurally. Each exercise asks you to commit to a reading first, then checks it against a verifiable source: Khan's published outlines or the corpus data itself.

Outline a discourse: al-'AsrPropose the structure of Surah 103 verse by verse, then compare your reading against the outline in Irfan Ahmad Khan's An Exercise in Understanding the Qur'an (2013). Spot the rootsShort surahs, one at a time. Tap the words you think share a triliteral root, then reveal the recurring root families computed from the Leeds corpus morphology.

Companion tool

Guided replayWatch any surah unfold: recitation verse by verse, recurring root families lighting up, and — where transcribed — Khan's outline appearing at each pivot. Best after you've attempted an exercise.

Planned

Khan's An Exercise in Understanding the Qur'an outlines all thirty of the final discourses (Surahs 85–114). The exercise engine is now data-driven: each new outline is a single entry in data/exercises.json, added as it is transcribed from the book — never paraphrased — so that every exercise remains traceable to its published source. Corrections and transcription help are welcome via the GitHub repository.

Divine Discourses is built in memory of Dr. Irfan Ahmad Khan (1931–2018).
