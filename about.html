About Divine Discourses

Divine Discourses is a platform for personal, evidence-based study of the Qur'an: read each surah as one coherent discourse, verify every claim against a labeled source, and build your own understanding directly from the text.

The site's coherence-based method draws on the Association of Quranic Understanding (quranicunderstanding.com) and the wider Farahi-Islahi-Mir tradition of reading each surah as a unified composition. The lineage referenced is intellectual, not a verified teacher-student chain.

Completely new to the Qur'an?

Welcome — no background is assumed here. The Qur'an is composed of 114 units called surahs, from a few lines to many pages long. This site's premise is that each surah reads best as one coherent discourse — a single address with a beginning, a claim, and a resolution — rather than as a string of isolated quotations. That is a claim you can test for yourself, whatever you believe: pick a short surah on the Read page (al-'Asr, at three verses, takes a minute), read it whole, and see whether it holds together as one argument. Every number and label you meet along the way links to its source, so nothing asks to be taken on trust.

About Dr. Khan

Dr. Irfan Ahmad Khan (1931–2018) was born in India. A science graduate of Aligarh Muslim University, he studied Qur'an and classical Arabic at Rampur before completing an MA in philosophy in 1958. He taught Western philosophy, Indian religions, and Muslim theology in India from 1958 to 1973, and edited the journal Islamic Thought in the early 1960s. He moved to the United States in 1974 for doctoral work at the University of Illinois at Chicago. He was a founding trustee of the Parliament of the World's Religions and of the International Committee for the Peace Council, and founding president of the World Council of Muslims for Interfaith Relations. He directed the Association of Quranic Understanding, where his weekly tafsir lessons and published writings developed a coherence-based reading of the Qur'an.

His published works in English include Reflections on the Qur'an (Islamic Foundation UK, 2005), an exegetical study of the first two chapters, and An Exercise in Understanding the Qur'an (Association for Quranic Understanding, 2013, ISBN 9781567447736), an outline study of the last thirty surahs that demonstrates his coherence-based method across a substantial portion of the text. The 2013 work carries a foreword by Mustansir Mir, author of Coherence in the Qur'an and a leading English-language scholar of the Farahi-Islahi exegetical tradition.

Why this exists

Khan emphasized that every reader builds their own direct and personal relationship with the Qur'an. He did not place himself within a single school. He drew on coherence-based reading developed by Farahi, Islahi, and Mir, on classical tafsir, on linguistic and historical scholarship, and on decades of his own reading. He taught that the Qur'an speaks to every human being, and that the believer's task is to engage it directly.

Every believer builds his or her own direct and personal relationship with the Book. From Irfan Ahmad Khan, Reflections on the Qur'an (Islamic Foundation UK, 2005). ●

This site gathers tools that support that engagement: roots, words, structure, patterns, frequency, and translation comparison. Every claim is labeled so readers can calibrate their confidence and triangulate independently.

Editorial standards

  • Every claim is labeled ●, ○, or ~.
  • No commentary is generated and no verse is told what it means. Sources are surfaced, citations linked, and the reader does the engagement. Where the site does curate — grouping roots under theme titles, offering short working glosses — that curation is editorial, and it is labeled Nuanced rather than presented as data.
  • Numerical claims that depend on a specific counting rule are marked with the counting note.
  • Sources excluded: polemic, anonymous, unsourced, and AI-generated commentary.

The name

"Divine Discourses" follows Khan's translation of khitab (plural khitabat) as God's address to the hearer, the unit he held to be the proper basis of Qur'anic reading.

Privacy

No analytics, no cookies, no tracking. Visitor preferences (depth level, translations, theme), reading progress, a cache of already-fetched verse text (so repeat reads load instantly), and any study notes you write on the Read page are saved in your browser's local storage and are never sent anywhere. Preferences, progress, and the cache can be cleared anytime with the Clear preferences button in Settings (the gear icon); notes are kept separately so that button cannot delete them — remove or export them from the notes panel itself.

Mission

Divine Discourses exists so that anyone — an independent student of the Qur'an, a teacher preparing a lesson, a skeptical reader who wants claims they can check — can engage the text directly, one surah at a time, with structural evidence they can verify rather than assertions they must trust. The outcome it works toward is simple to state: readers equipped to test the coherence of a discourse for themselves, with every supporting figure traceable to a named source.

The site is built to last cheaply and openly: static pages, data bundled with the code, near-zero hosting cost, everything under an open-source license. It is also honest about what it is today — a young project with a single maintainer. Against that, its method and every build step are documented in a public maintainer's guide written so that any future contributor could carry it forward.

Contribute

What this project needs is not money but careful work, and three kinds especially. Corrections — if any claim, citation, or count on this site is wrong, say so via GitHub issues; every correction is logged publicly on the changelog. Transcription — Khan's published outlines cover the last thirty surahs, and most still await careful transcription into the exercises. Scholarly review — if you work in Qur'anic studies or Arabic linguistics and are willing to review the site's method pages against their sources, that scrutiny would be received with gratitude and acted on in public.
