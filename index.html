How we verify

Published research claims expose separate source, reproduction, and dependency statuses—not one undifferentiated promise of certainty. Every claim carries a label: ● Verified, ○ Pending, or ~ Nuanced.

Four worked examples (Verified and Nuanced)

● Verified

The five most frequent roots in the Qur'an: a-l-h (God, 2,851), q-w-l (say, 1,722), k-w-n (be, 1,390), r-b-b (Lord, 980), a-m-n (believe, 879).

Traced to the Leeds Quranic Arabic Corpus (Dukes 2009–2017), corpus.quran.com dictionary. Full table on Numbers.

● Verified

Farahi's Mufradat al-Qur'an and Niẓām al-Qur'an are foundational to the coherence-based reading school.

Independently corroborated by Mustansir Mir's 1986 monograph on Islahi's nazm method. Full citations on Sources.

~ Nuanced

Root y-w-m (day) occurs 405 times in the Leeds corpus. Widely circulated totals such as 365 or 475 count selected surface or compound forms instead; this site has not reproduced those totals.

Only the 405 root total is verified here. The other totals are cautions about changing the unit of count, not endorsed findings. See Numbers.

● Verified

Of every root it ever shares a verse with, r-ḥ-m (mercy) appears most often beside gh-f-r (forgiveness) — together in 91 verses, far ahead of any other pairing.

Counted from the bundled co-occurrence tables (Leeds v0.4); the same pairing tops the list from gh-f-r's side too. Explore on Roots.

Purpose, audience, and scope

Divine Discourses is a tool for direct, evidence-based study of the Qur'an: roots, morphology, structural patterns, and cross-references with provenance attached. It is for readers who want to engage the text without an intermediary, and for students who want claims they can trace.
