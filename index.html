What this site does

Discourses lets you read any verse of the Qur'an alongside multiple English translations, look up any of the 1,642 triliteral roots, see how Qur'anic words derive from those roots, compare roots across chronological periods, and trace recurring structural patterns. Every claim carries a verification label so you can calibrate confidence and triangulate independently.

Three depth levels

Simple

Verse text, translations, audio. The reading layer.

Scholar

Adds word-by-word morphology, root links, related verses, and period distribution. The analytical layer.

Encyclopedic

Adds structural pattern notes and full provenance. The reference layer.

Change depth anytime via the gear icon in the top right. Your selection persists for this tab only. No tracking, no cookies.

A suggested first visit

  1. Open al-Fatihah 1:1 and read the basmala in multiple translations at Simple depth.
  2. Switch to Scholar depth (gear icon) and reload the same verse to see word-by-word morphology with root links.
  3. Visit the Roots page and click any row to see how that root distributes across the Meccan and Medinan periods.
  4. Use Compare to set two roots side by side. Try r-ḥ-m (mercy) against E-*-b (punishment) for a thematic contrast.
  5. Read the About page for context on Dr. Khan and the coherence-based approach that shapes this site.

When you have done the first visit, try Khan's outline of Surah al-Asr. This is the coherence-based reading method this site honors, demonstrated live on the shortest meaningful surah.

Verification labels

Verified from primary corpus or canonical edition. Pending awaiting triangulation. Nuanced the counting method or interpretation has known variation; see source for detail.

Run the prompts on Validation against ChatGPT, Gemini, or Grok to triangulate any Pending claim into Verified, or surface the nuance that keeps it labeled accordingly.
