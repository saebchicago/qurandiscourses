# Video walkthrough script — "How to study with Divine Discourses"

A ready-to-record script for a screen-capture training video (~10 minutes).
Each scene lists the URL to show, the actions to perform on screen, and
suggested narration. Record with the browser at a comfortable zoom; pause
a beat after each click so viewers can follow.

Also usable as-is for a live demo or a written tutorial with screenshots.

---

## Scene 1 — What this site is (0:00–1:00)
**Show:** `/` (home), scroll slowly.
**Do:** Nothing yet; let the page speak.
**Say:** "Divine Discourses is a study platform built in memory of Dr. Irfan
Ahmad Khan, for one purpose: direct, personal engagement with the Qur'an.
It is not a search engine and it generates no commentary. It does two
things: it puts the text's own structure in front of you — roots, words,
patterns, frequencies — and it labels every factual claim so you can
check it yourself. You do the reading; the site orders the evidence."

## Scene 2 — Ask box (1:00–2:00)
**Show:** the Ask box on home.
**Do:** Type `2:255` → Enter (lands on Read). Go back. Type `mercy` →
Enter (lands on Roots filtered to r-ḥ-m). Go back. Type `رحم` → Enter
(Roots again — Arabic script works). Go back. Type `business` → Enter
(lands on the Trade & wealth theme).
**Say:** "One box, five kinds of input: a verse reference, a surah name,
a root — in Latin letters or Arabic script — an English word, and a life
theme. It routes you to the right tool."

## Scene 3 — Read, depths, and settings (2:00–3:30)
**Show:** `/read.html?s=103&a=1-3`.
**Do:** Read at Simple. Press `2` (Study) — word-by-word morphology
appears. Open the gear (bottom right): show Depth, Palette (switch to
Sage and back), Theme. Then open the per-verse "N selected" button to
show the translation picker. Point at the gear panel's privacy line.
**Say:** "Three depths: Simple is just reading; Study adds morphology,
roots, and related verses; Encyclopedic adds full provenance. Keyboard
1-2-3 switches anywhere. Settings also has palettes and themes — and
notice: preferences stay in your browser; nothing is tracked."

## Scene 4 — The verification system (3:30–5:00)
**Show:** `/numbers.html`.
**Do:** Hover a ● badge (tooltip), then click it — the Chicago citation
popover opens. Then open `/validation.html`, scroll the worked examples.
**Say:** "This is the heart of the method. Every statistic carries a
badge: Verified means confirmed from the primary source cited — click
and you get the full citation. Pending means awaiting a second source.
Nuanced means scholars genuinely count differently, and the note tells
you why. The Validation page shows worked examples and gives you prompts
to cross-examine our claims with whatever tools you trust — but only the
primary source verifies anything."

## Scene 5 — Roots, the engine room (5:00–6:30)
**Show:** `/roots.html?q=r-h-m`.
**Do:** Detail opens automatically. Scroll: count, Makki/Madani bar,
distribution, derived forms (click "Show all"), co-occurring roots
(click gh-f-r to hop roots), verse chips (click one → Read).
**Say:** "Arabic organizes meaning around three-letter roots. Here is
mercy: 339 occurrences, its grammatical forms, where it clusters, and
what it travels with — forgiveness is its most frequent companion. Every
verse reference is a link. This entire page runs from data bundled with
the site, from the Leeds corpus, so you can verify any number."

## Scene 6 — Themes: from life to text (6:30–7:45)
**Show:** `/themes.html`, jump to Forgiveness.
**Do:** Show the root chips, the computed key passages (open 24:22), the
"top two side by side" Compare link, the self-study kit.
**Say:** "Themes are gateways: pick forgiveness, marriage, trade, peace.
Each maps to its root families and to passages where that vocabulary
clusters — computed from the corpus, not hand-picked. The label is
honest: these are lexical starting points, not a complete index of the
theme. The study kit turns each gateway into your own analysis."

## Scene 7 — Exercises (7:45–9:00)
**Show:** `/exercise-roots.html?s=109`.
**Do:** Tap a few words you think repeat, click Reveal — highlights and
the score appear. Then show `/exercise-asr.html` briefly: propose, then
Reveal the outline.
**Say:** "The exercises make you commit before you check. Spot-the-roots
trains your eye on repetition — al-Kafirun lights up around ʿ-b-d,
worship. The al-'Asr exercise asks you to outline the discourse yourself,
then compares your reading with the outline in Dr. Khan's published book
— cited, never invented."

## Scene 8 — Study paths + where to go deeper (9:00–10:00)
**Show:** `/paths.html`, then `/sources.html` briefly.
**Do:** Scroll path 1; end on the Sources bibliography.
**Say:** "Study paths chain the tools into a method: follow one root end
to end; profile a surah before reading it; trace any claim back to its
source. And everything the site rests on is in the open — a full
Chicago-style bibliography, the data files, and the code. Nothing here
asks for your trust; it shows its work. That is the method: read
directly, verify everything, and build your own relationship with the
Book."
