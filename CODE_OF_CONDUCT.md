# Code of conduct

This project studies a scripture that people hold sacred, alongside
scholarship about it. Both deserve care, and so do the people doing the
work. The rules are short because they are meant to be kept.

## Expectations

- **Argue from evidence.** The site's whole method is claims traceable
  to named sources. Hold contributions and criticism to the same
  standard: cite the corpus, the page, the edition. "I believe" is a
  starting point, not an argument.
- **Respect the text and its readers.** Contributors and readers span
  traditions and levels of practice, and include people of no
  tradition. Polemic for or against any tradition, sect, or religion is
  off-topic everywhere in this project.
- **Respect the scholarship.** Quoted scholars (Khan, Islahi, Farahi,
  Mir, and others) are quoted verbatim and attributed. Disagree with a
  reading as much as you like; do not misquote, truncate to distort, or
  present paraphrase as quotation.
- **Be direct and courteous with people.** Critique work, not persons.
  No harassment, no slurs, no personal attacks, no dogpiling. Assume
  the other person wants the project to be right.
- **Stay within licensing.** Do not contribute text or data you do not
  have the right to license under the terms in CONTRIBUTING.md.

## Scope

These expectations apply in issues, pull requests, discussions, review
comments, and any space representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report conduct problems through a GitHub issue, or privately through
the contact route on the About page if the report concerns a
maintainer. The maintainer will acknowledge within two working days
(the same service level the accessibility statement promises), and may
edit, hide, lock, or remove contributions and, for repeated or severe
violations, block participants. Enforcement decisions are recorded in
the issue that prompted them whenever privacy allows.
